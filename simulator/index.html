Dynamic Nucleosynthesis

This is a simplified, interactive model of how oxygen-carbon shell mergers contribute to the radioactive 40K in planets. Carbon is ingested from an upper shell and it mixes into the much hotter oxygen shell, releasing neutrons, protons, and alphas on the way that produce 40K. When the star explodes, it contributes to the galaxy, and then ends up condensed in planets and heats up their mantles. Watch as you change the mixing physics how planets respond.

What the model does

Convective-reactive sites are environments where the time to convectively mix material and for a reaction to occur become equal, which can be described by Da = τmix/τburn. When Da ≪ 1, mixing is fast enough to homogenize the shell, and when Da ≫ 1, isotopes react before they can mix. However, in the oxygen-carbon shell merger burning occurs at similar timescales as mixing. As you change the mixing prescription, see how the reactions change and the 40K produced as well. The ingested material releases protons, neutrons, and alphas that allow 38Ar(p,γ)39K(n,γ)40K (Issa et al. 2026). At the same time, 40K is being destroyed at the hottest temperatures where 40K(p,n)40Ca dominates. The ratio of 40K/39K depends on the exact balance of mixing to burning. When the star explodes, its potassium enriches the gas of the Milky Way, and the [K/Fe] of the Galaxy depends on how many massive stars merge and how their mergers mix. 40K decays with a half-life of 1.25 Gyr, so the 40K/K of the gas reflects the balance between recent enrichment and decay. When rocky planets form, they inherit the 40K of the gas and as it decays it releases heat into the mantle.
